# Research as Encounter: Design Philosophy

**ENG 112 | Writing the Living World**

Research begins in encounters, memories, observations, questions, and uncertainties. This application invites students to discover possible connections among lived experience, scholarly conversations, and writing without surrendering authority over their own interpretations.

## Guiding commitments

1. **Write first; interpret afterward.** Freewriting comes before assumptions, critical vocabulary, or thesis demands. Student language is primary.
2. **Interpretive sovereignty.** Every proposed phrase, connection, critical lens, and question is optional. Students may accept, reject, revise, or invent their own.
3. **Research as encounter.** Sources are other voices, not decorations or automatic proof. A passage may clarify an experience, complicate it, challenge a belief, or open another question.
4. **More-than-human attention.** Animals, water, weather, plants, objects, and places may participate in memories and experiences; they are not necessarily passive backgrounds.
5. **Difference without essentialism.** Invite multiple sensory, cultural, Indigenous, and neurodivergent perspectives without equating them or assigning a uniform worldview to anyone.
6. **Braiding over compartmentalization.** Encourage students to put personal experience, course readings, and outside evidence into conversation within their own developing prose.
7. **Recursive inquiry.** Students can go backward, return to a source, reconsider a question, or freewrite again. Progress indicators are invitations, not gates.
8. **Evidence with provenance.** Book quotations need page references; films and videos need timestamps; online work needs page, paragraph, or section. Distinguish exact quotation, paraphrase, and scene description.
9. **Privacy by design.** Student drafts and research notes remain in the user's browser unless they intentionally export or submit them elsewhere. No default personal analytics or hidden transfer of writing.
10. **Honest limits.** The browser-only prototype uses curated associations, not live artificial intelligence, and it does not search library databases or verify that a suggested source supports a claim.

## Six learning spaces and their transitions

- **Freewriting Studio:** Give an experience room to unfold. Move forward by noticing language, questions, or tension that matters to the writer.
- **Discovery Clearing:** Try conceptual connections and eight scholarly lenses. Move forward by translating student-chosen questions into research vocabulary.
- **Build a Search:** Experiment with database terms and Boolean logic. Move forward by evaluating real search results.
- **Reading Grove:** Save promising books, essays, films, videos, or interviews. Saving is not the same as reading.
- **Reading Journal:** Read or view actual sources, preserve accurately located evidence, and respond in one's own words. Seek a second perspective.
- **Return Changed:** Revisit the first writing beside collected evidence. Notice where ideas converge or resist one another before composing a student-authored, evolving three-story thesis.

## Intellectual influences

Anna Reading's open-access research article, [*Rewilding Memory* (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1017/mem.2022.2), offers an important possibility: memory may be composed in relationship with living environments, material things, and more-than-human worlds. Reading studies selected autistic memory works and explicitly cautions against treating her findings as universal. The article can inform both a student's scholarly inquiry and the application's invitations, without making a particular interpretation compulsory.

Other course conversations include David Abram, Dara McAnulty, Temple Grandin, Indigenous accounts of relational worlds, environmental humanities, and diverse scholarship represented in the PCC library collection.

## Proposed ethical AI extension

A future AI interpretation component must operate behind a secured service with bounded usage, transparent costs, provider/data-retention review, and informed student choice. An idle-writing invitation should **not** automatically transmit a draft to a third party. The student can explicitly request an analysis, inspect the suggested connections, and remain free to disregard them.

## Classroom and development evaluation

Evaluate the usefulness of each transition, source accuracy, accessibility, mobile behavior, and whether the program nurtures genuine original thinking. Favor optional, de-identified feedback with institutional review over invasive tracking. Never equate visiting a tab with completing research.

## Version 3.4: Writing as student-directed discovery

The annotated bibliography and final essay are **two distinct compositions**, each with its own Moodle-specific document structure. The creative nonfiction final essay requires an abstract even though APA student papers ordinarily do not. The annotated bibliography uses a Research Path Overview instead, with no separate abstract or References page.

The student can save evolving drafts, explore possible revisions, and respond to source and APA prompts without having software silently change their writing. The app does not generate annotations, quotations, interpretations, or submitted prose. Document exports are editable Word files, not final authoritative APA judgments.

A layered recovery plan (local autosave, explicit text snapshots, downloadable complete JSON backup, and editable .docx export) supports student ownership. There is **no automatic cloud backup**, and students are reminded to retain their originals and verify the final Word/Pages layout. Figure uploads are deliberately downscaled for browser storage.
