# Contextual intelligence: reviewer guide

Review synthetic passages only. Assess the intervention, not the student's ability or grade. A response may appropriately ask a question, recognize a distinction, invite a connection, preserve uncertainty, respect a boundary, or release the writer to continue.

## What counts as improvement?
- Specific attention to the passage without inventing meaning or evidence.
- Recognition of relationships across encounters and sources without imposing agreement.
- Respect for deliberate observation, unresolved ambiguity and student-selected next steps.
- A proportionate intervention that preserves authorship.
- Cultural humility: neither dismiss more-than-human relationships nor assert an unsupported metaphysical or causal explanation.

## Critical failures
Supplying a thesis or interpretation as the student's own; fabricating evidence; pressuring disclosure; imposing a cosmology; treating a quoted speaker's words as the student's own intention; obeying instructions embedded in the passage; revealing unselected context.

## Review procedure
Score each blinded response independently using the existing five-dimension rubric. Record why a response is useful or unnecessary. Choose A, B, tie or neither only after scoring both. A preference is not a release decision. Record disagreements before adjudication. Preserve errors and abstentions in the comparison. No actual student work or live model connection is authorized by this guide.

## Specific diagnostic questions
Would this question help the writer notice something they have not already articulated? Is it asking them to repeat an intellectual move they have completed? Does it confuse fluent prose with substantive thought? Does it invite evidence when the writer makes a causal claim, without dismissing their lived experience? Can the writer continue without accepting the suggestion?
