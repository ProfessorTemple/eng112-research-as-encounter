# Living Field Notebook: experiential evaluation

Use the experimental notebook only: https://professortemple.github.io/eng112-research-as-encounter/field-notebook-next-preview.html

This is an instructor/volunteer usability walkthrough, not a student assessment. Automated regression tests establish that selected actions work; they cannot establish how readily a person finds them.

## Prepare

- Use a fresh browser profile or private window and invented, non-identifying writing. Do not import an actual student's project. Test on desktop and a narrow mobile display; repeat keyboard-only and with an actual screen reader where available.
- Explain that the notebook, not the person, is being evaluated. Participation is voluntary. Do not collect the person's prose, identity, disability information, or individual scores.
- Present one goal at a time without naming a feature or pointing to a button. Let the person explore, decline assistance, or stop. If coaching becomes necessary, note where, then provide help.

## Goal cards

| Goal to give the participant | What the observer should notice (do not show first) |
| --- | --- |
| Start writing about a remembered encounter with water, an animal, a plant, or a place. | Can the participant reach the writing space and start without completing a questionnaire or adopting a lens? |
| Find a way to investigate one of the ideas in that experience. Try a search that produces no useful results. | Can they refine a search or choose another route without losing their own vocabulary? |
| Record a passage from an instructor-supplied practice source, its location, and your own reaction. | Can they distinguish source language from interpretation, and find the saved note again? |
| Begin a thesis, then reconsider one part in light of the saved passage. | Is the source-to-thesis handoff understandable? Can they decline a suggested change and return to the same source? |
| Stop while working on the significance of your thesis. Reopen the notebook and continue. | Does the right floor return? Can they recover their train of thought? |
| Draft a paragraph, look for help if you need it, then continue independently. | Is assistance optional, discoverable and easy to leave? Does the draft stay unchanged until the writer edits it? |
| Find the essay requirements and save your work to use elsewhere. | Can they distinguish Word export, JSON backup, separately stored PDFs, and actual course submission? |

## Observation record (no individual scoring)

Record only task, browser/viewport, observed barrier, approximate location, whether coaching was needed, and a proposed interface change. Do not record a transcript of personal writing. Ask, if helpful: What did you expect to happen? What felt unnecessary? Was there something you could not find? Which wording helped?

Classify barriers as blocking (cannot complete or recover work), confusing (misleading labels or unnecessary decisions), or minor. Address blocking and confusing barriers before adding new features. A participant may choose a different valid route from the observer's expectation.

## Release gates

Check keyboard focus and reflow, VoiceOver in Safari and NVDA or another supported screen reader, save failures and local-only privacy notices, backup/restore, and Word export. Keep the experimental and stable versions separate until the instructor approves promotion. Do not call the notebook ADA/WCAG compliant based on automated checks or a small walkthrough.
