**Regular application (unchanged):**

## Why we are testing
Evaluate whether the additional guidance helps students move from **specific observation** to **arguable interpretation** to **earned significance**, while retaining a record of how source work and personal encounters changed their thinking. A completed checklist is not evidence of mastery.

## What the preview adds
- One short practice exercise: distinguish Story Two (a debatable interpretation) from Story One (observation) and Story Three (wider implication).
- Five **self-assessment statements** that students check only if their actual thesis demonstrates them. No automated grade is assigned.
- A "thoughtful reader" conversation about a possible alternative interpretation, verifiable source evidence, and the connection between the second and third stories.
- A **dated thesis history**, where each newly saved version has its own associated self-review and reader responses. Existing saved versions are not overwritten.
- Separate browser storage key `eng112-research-thesis-preview-v35`. The main student application continues using `eng112-research-encounter-v2`. The preview does **not** load analytics.js.
- Original research and writing features remain available in this preview, but **do not use it as your only assignment copy**. Back up the preview separately if needed.

## Instructor test in ten minutes
1. Open the preview and work with an invented experience (e.g., a dog staying near you when you are sad).
2. In **Explore Connections**, write a research question. Continue to **Return Changed**.
3. Complete the **three-story fields**: first a specific observed scene; second a contestable interpretation; third an implication that follows from that interpretation.
4. Use **Bring my three stories together**. Read and improve the resulting prose. It only joins the student's own sentences.
5. Answer the mini exercise, check the five self-assessment items honestly, and respond to the three reader questions.
6. Click **Save this stage of my thinking**. Verify that the version has a dated entry and an archived self-review.
7. Revise the second story after considering a source, interview, or new keyword. Explain what changed, then save a second version.
8. Compare both versions. Refresh the page and verify the draft, self-review, and history remain.
9. Test the same flow using only a keyboard (Tab/Shift+Tab; Space on checkboxes; Enter on buttons). Listen with Mac VoiceOver and use mobile screen magnification if available.
10. Return to the **regular** application and confirm its browser-saved writing is unchanged.

## Mini evaluation rubric (instructor only, 0–8; diagnostic, not necessarily a course grade)

| Criterion | 0 · Not yet evident | 1 · Developing | 2 · Clear |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First Story: Observation** | No particular lived or documented observation | Topic or scene present but vague | Specific, truthful, situated encounter or pattern |
| **Second Story: Interpretation** | Repeats description or offers an unsupported assertion | Some meaning suggested but not yet debatable/defensible | Meaningful, arguable interpretation that can be investigated |
| **Third Story: Significance** | Generic moral or sweeping claim | Larger idea visible but connection weak | Broader implication follows clearly and proportionately from Story Two |
| **Encounter with sources** | No evidence that inquiry changed or informed the thinking | Sources or vocabulary appear but are loosely connected | Evidence, counterevidence, interviews, or new concepts materially complicate/refine the claim |

For an initial and revised thesis from the same student, record the 0–8 score separately, note what changed, and ask the student: **What does your thesis now understand that your original question did not yet explain?** Do not assume a higher score implies deeper discovery unless the revision itself supports that judgment.

## Student pilot questions
- Which of the three stories was hardest to develop, and why?
- What research or memory changed your interpretation?
- Did the app ever suggest that there was only one correct interpretation?
- Did the self-check help you find a genuine weakness, or did it simply feel like a task to complete?
- Could you use the page and all controls with your preferred device and accessibility settings?

## Boundaries and release criteria
- Start with **three to five volunteer students** or instructor-generated sample texts; do not collect private reflective drafts through analytics.
- Keep a copy of student work only with appropriate permission, following college policy; use anonymized excerpts where possible.
- Check keyboard navigation, screen-reader labels, contrast, mobile zoom, and recovery from a JSON backup.
- Students should be able to distinguish the **interpretive second story** in their own language.
- Students should be able to explain why their third story grows from the second, rather than leaping to a universal lesson.
- Students should identify actual evidence or meaningful uncertainty that complicates an earlier thesis.
- The preview must not alter the production site or drafts, and no quiz/self-review responses are automatically graded or transmitted.
- Promote to production only after the learning and accessibility tests are satisfactory.
