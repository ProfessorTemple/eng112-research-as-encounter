Build a research search

Academic database searching is a conversation with uncertainty. Try a search, look at the results, and revise your vocabulary. The three original ideas are separate concepts joined with AND; use OR only for alternatives or synonyms.

Open the ENG 112 PCC Library guide ↗ (internet and PCC login may be required).

Search operators: AND, OR, NOT, quotes, parentheses

AND narrows by requiring both concepts. OR broadens by allowing alternatives. NOT excludes but can hide important results. Quotes search an exact phrase where the database supports it, and parentheses keep alternatives grouped. Database behavior varies.

Example: ("grandmothers" OR "intergenerational knowledge") AND gardening

Too many or too few results?

Too many: add one specific concept using AND, or use a phrase in quotation marks. Too few: remove one concept, add synonyms with OR, or try broader terms. Check article abstracts for better vocabulary. The program does not query PCC databases or verify subject headings.
