Build a research search

Academic database searching is a conversation with uncertainty. Try a search, look at the results, and revise your vocabulary. The three original ideas are separate concepts joined with AND; use OR only for alternatives or synonyms.

Open the ENG 112 PCC Library guide ↗ (internet and PCC login may be required).

Search operators: AND, OR, NOT, quotes, parentheses

AND narrows by requiring both concepts. OR broadens by allowing alternatives. NOT excludes but can hide important results. Quotes search an exact phrase where the database supports it, and parentheses keep alternatives grouped. Database behavior varies.

Example: ("grandmothers" OR "intergenerational knowledge") AND gardening

Too many or too few results?

Too many: add one specific concept using AND, or use a phrase in quotation marks. Too few: remove one concept, add synonyms with OR, or try broader terms. Check article abstracts for better vocabulary. The program does not query PCC databases or verify subject headings.

Find sources: the Reading Grove

Books, essays, films, and recorded conversations can all enter your inquiry. Read or watch the source itself, then bring one specific passage or moment to your Reading Journal. These are starting points, not evidence that a source agrees with you.

Access matters: Course Reserve books are requested at the circulation desk, General Collection books are on the library shelves, and PCC e-books require library access. A recommendation is not a live availability check.
Start with our primary course text: Diary of a Young Naturalist by Dara McAnulty. It is part of ENG 112 coursework, not an additional PCC catalog holding. Every inquiry can return to McAnulty's attention to lived encounters, sensory difference, and the more-than-human world.

The Reading Journal

Finding a source begins the conversation. Now read, watch, or listen and return with specific evidence. Save a passage, scene, or idea, then ask how it meets, challenges, or changes your own experience.

A note about citations: For books, record page numbers and edition where relevant. For films and videos, record a timestamp such as 00:24:30. For web essays, record a section or paragraph if there are no pages. Quote only words you have verified in the source. A scene description is not a quotation.
